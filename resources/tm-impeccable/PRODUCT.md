# PRODUCT — Tuinmaximaal

> Strategy context (who / what / why) for the Tuinmaximaal storefront. Pairs with
> [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), which carries the visual system (color, type, components, motion).
> Read both before any design work. Impeccable design skills use this as their grounding.

## Register

**Product surface — design serves the product.** This is commerce UI (catalog, product
pages, cart, checkout) where the job, not the decoration, leads. Clarity and confidence come
first; brand flourish is deliberate and restrained. Homepage, category landings, and campaign
pages may take a slightly more expressive, brand-forward treatment, but the default register
is "design serves the shopping task."

## Audience

Dutch consumers researching and buying **garden and outdoor structures** — verandas, garden
rooms, canopies, carports and related high-ticket outdoor products. Concretely: *a homeowner
on the sofa comparing veranda configurations across several evenings, weighing a four-figure
purchase, who needs to feel certain about specs, fit, delivery, and that the company stands
behind it before committing.* Mixed desktop and mobile; often multiple sessions before
purchase; frequently mid-to-late funnel and risk-aware.

## What & Why — the job to be done

> "Help me choose the right structure for my garden with confidence, and make a large,
> complex purchase feel safe, well-organized, and straightforward."

The product wins by **removing doubt**: clear specifications, configurators, honest pricing,
strong social proof (reviews, USPs), guarantees, and transparent delivery. Every screen
should answer the shopper's next question before they have to go looking for it.

## Brand voice

**Trustworthy · solid · helpful.** Grounded and expert, never flashy. Authority comes from
clarity, quality imagery, and proof — not from loud decoration or pressure tactics. Friendly
and guiding in tone (the way a knowledgeable shop assistant would be), reassuring at the
moments that matter.

Primary emotion to engineer at decision points (PDP, cart, checkout): **confidence.** Reduce
the anxiety of a big-ticket purchase by surfacing reviews, USPs, guarantees, delivery clarity,
and clean specs exactly where doubt would otherwise creep in.

## Visual references (the feel to aim for)

Named products, not adjectives — aim for *how these feel*, not a copy of their look:

- **Coolblue** — the benchmark for trust and confidence in Dutch e-commerce: explicit
  guidance, helpful tone, service and proof cues woven through the whole funnel, and a calm,
  legible interface. This is the emotional target.
- **Hornbach** — robust, project-oriented, practical. Treats a big outdoor build as a
  manageable project; confident with specs, dimensions, and "here's everything you need."
- **IKEA** — accessible and exceptionally well-organized: clear pricing, guided choices,
  inspirational room/garden context, and structure that makes a complex catalog feel simple.

The throughline: **large, mainstream, dependable retail that earns trust through clarity and
organization** — not boutique minimalism or luxury editorial.

## Anti-references (explicitly NOT this)

- **No generic AI-slop look** — no cyan-on-dark, no purple/blue gradients, no gradient text,
  no decorative glassmorphism, no endless identical card grids, no colored `border-left`
  accent stripes on cards/alerts.
- **No drift from brand tokens** — stay within the greens / orange / sand palette and
  Articulat CF. No new font families, no off-brand colors. (See [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).)
- **Not heavy or dark** — keep it light and open; never dark mode, never visually heavy.
- **No loud discount/coupon styling** — promotions stay tasteful and on-brand, never
  aggressive sale-banner aesthetics.

> The `CLAUDE.md` "Academic Journal on Vellum / Anthropic Serif" sheet on the Desktop is
> **not** this project's design system and should be ignored for Tuinmaximaal work.

## Design principles

1. **Confidence over persuasion.** Answer the next doubt (specs, reviews, guarantees,
   delivery) before asking for the click. Proof and clarity beat pressure.
2. **Organized = trustworthy.** Like IKEA and Hornbach, make a complex catalog feel
   manageable through structure, clear pricing, and guided choices.
3. **Green structures, orange acts.** Deep green carries layout and hierarchy; orange and
   lime CTAs are reserved for primary actions and true highlights. Accents work because rare.
4. **Warm, light, and roomy.** Sand/beige surfaces and generous space keep a big purchase
   calm. Light theme always.
5. **Token discipline + accessible by default (WCAG 2.2 AA).** Build with the existing
   semantic classes and token scale; meet AA contrast, visible focus, reduced-motion, and
   adequate touch targets.
